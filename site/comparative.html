Comparative paragraph: elicitation and lint

The FIELD paragraph is elicited with four questions, then the linter rejects the drafts that would create legal exposure. These rejections are the real output of the sign-off check.

The four elicitation questions

  1. 1 What did all shortlisted applicants demonstrate that most applicants did not?
  2. 2 What concrete evidence proved it (project, metric, tool, scale)?
  3. 3 What did a strong application make visible within 30 seconds?
  4. 4 What did many applicants assume mattered that actually didn't?

The approved paragraph v1.0

Shortlisted applicants demonstrated assessment of a deteriorating patient, escalation to a registrar within their first hour, and documentation a covering clinician could act on. The clearest evidence was a de-identified case note attached to the application. Applicants did not need a post-graduate qualification or a specific employer to be considered.

Signed off by the Hiring Manager and Legal; rendered identically to every FIELD candidate.

Shortlisted applicants were the strongest cultural fit with the team.

Candidates who weren't shortlisted typically lacked the depth we needed.

comparative paragraph failed sign-off: implied candidate deficit: "lacked"; implied candidate deficit: "typically lacked"

Rejected draft

Shortlisted applicants had 5+ years experience in a similar role.

comparative paragraph failed sign-off: years-of-experience bar: "years of experience"; years-of-experience bar: "a numeric years requirement"

The lint rules

  • Relative superiority claim — "more experience than", "stronger than most".
  • Implied individual deficit — "lacked", "typically lacked".
  • Subjective proxy — "cultural fit", "right attitude".
  • Years-of-experience bar — any numeric years requirement.
